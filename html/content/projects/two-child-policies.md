# Two-child policies in India

Did laws barring parents of more than two children from local office and government employment reduce third births, and did they weigh more heavily on poor families or families without sons?

Several Indian states adopted two-child norms that penalized households exceeding a two-child limit. Using two-stage matched samples from the National Family Health Survey across three adoption waves, I estimate the effect of these policies on progression from a second to a third birth in the short and longer run.Triple-difference estimates show no evidence that the policies operated differently by sibling sex composition or household poverty. Son preference and poverty shaped third births independently of the policy, in adopting and comparison states, before and after. The result points to a different kind of policy failure: an instrument with too little force to engage either mechanism.

- **Data** NFHS
- **Methods** Difference-in-differences, matching
