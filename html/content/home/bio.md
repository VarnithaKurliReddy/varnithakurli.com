I am a final year Phd candidate in Environmental Studies department at CU Boulder. I have a background in economics and mathematics, with graduate training in logic and research methodology, and a decade of applied work on forests, water, cities and disasters. One question connects my training and research: **what happens to people when the systems around them are shocked, and how can data (satellite and survey) help with adaptation and recovery from these shocks?** My dissertation brings that question to population change, tracing how climate hazards and population policies shape fertility, migration and mortality across India, Bangladesh and the world. My goal is to keep generating evidence and producing research that is rigorous enough for science and practical enough for the people making decisions on the ground. I am deeply grateful for working on challenges that may have made a difference, somewhere in the world. To have had the opportunity to serve at the World Bank and pursue research I truly believe in is a privilege.

I am affiliated with the [Better Planet Laboratory](https://betterplanetlab.com/) and the [Environment and Behavior Lab](https://carricolab.weebly.com/) at CU Boulder.

Earlier, I was a Water Data Specialist at the [World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/), where I continue to provide analytical and advisory support as a Data Scientist Consultant. I received an M.Sc. in Logic, Computation and Methodology from Carnegie Mellon University and a B.A. with honors in Economics and Mathematics from the University of Massachusetts Amherst.
